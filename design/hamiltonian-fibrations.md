# Hamiltonian fibrations

This is the detailed design of part 54 of `DESIGN.md`, *Hamiltonian
fibrations*. It covers one milestone of `atlas/symplectic-fibrations.md`,
*Characterization of Hamiltonian fibrations*, in the corrected form with the
torsion condition that the page states: McDuff–Salamon [McDuffSalamon2017],
§6.5, Theorem 6.5.3 with Remark 6.5.4, and the correction of
Lalonde–McDuff [LalondeMcDuff2005]. The implication 3 ⇒ 1 is proved by a
shorter route than the book's, with no triangulation of the base (see
*Decisions*).

All names below are in the namespace `SymplecticGeometry` unless they are
written with another one. Paths of new files are under `SymplecticGeometry/`.
Tau Ceti paths are relative to `.lake/packages/TauCeti/TauCeti/`, as in
`DESIGN.md`. All statements about Mathlib and Tau Ceti were checked against
the versions pinned when this file was written (Tau Ceti `c9d170eed5a8`).
The names of *Parallel transport*, *Symplectic fibrations* and *Flux
homomorphism* are those that their design files give
(`design/parallel-transport.md`, `design/symplectic-fibrations.md`,
`design/flux-homomorphism.md`). *Coupling forms* has no design file yet. Its
results are named below by what they say. The building issues name the
declarations of that part's design file once it is merged.

## Setting and notation

- **The fibration.** As in *Symplectic fibrations*: `π : M → B` with a
  smooth atlas `A : TauCeti.MapFiber.Atlas F π`, `hA : A.IsContMDiff ∞ IB IF IM`,
  and a fibre form `σ : SmoothTwoForm IF F` with `σ.IsSymplectic`, with
  `hS : A.HasStructureGroup (TauCeti.SmoothForm.diffeomorphStabilizer σ)`.
  The manifolds are boundaryless and Hausdorff. `σ` is also read as an
  element of `TauCeti.closedForms IF F 2` where Tau Ceti's flux needs one;
  below, `Γ_σ` is `TauCeti.fluxGroup σ`.
- **The milestone's hypotheses.** `[CompactSpace M]`, `[ConnectedSpace M]`,
  `[CompactSpace F]` and `[ConnectedSpace F]`, as on the page, whose
  conventions make the total space connected and the fibre connected. Then
  `B = π '' M` is compact and connected, and `π` is proper (Tau Ceti's
  `TauCeti.isProperMap_of_forall_exists_trivialization`), so parallel
  transport exists.
- **Loops.** A smooth loop of `B` is `γ : ℝ → B` with
  `ContMDiff 𝓘(ℝ) IB ∞ γ` and `Function.Periodic γ 1`. Holonomy is the
  transport from time `0` to time `1`. A continuous loop
  `Path b b` is replaced by a smooth one in its class with Tau Ceti's
  `Path.exists_homotopic_contMDiff_extend`
  (`Geometry/Manifold/SmoothApprox.lean`), reparametrized by
  `Real.smoothTransition` so that it is constant near the ends and extends
  periodically.
- **First homology.** $H_1(B;\mathbb Z)$ is
  `((singularChains ℤ).obj (TopCat.of B)).homology 1`, as in Tau Ceti's
  `TauCeti.HurewiczOne`. The class of a loop is
  `H₁AddEquivHomology _ (loopClass γ)`. A homomorphism on the fundamental
  group with abelian values goes to $H_1$ by Tau Ceti's
  `TauCeti.HurewiczOne.homologyLift` (`Hurewicz/One/HomologyLift.lean`),
  which needs `[PathConnectedSpace B]`. Torsion is Mathlib's
  `AddCommGroup.torsion`. $H_1(B;\mathbb Z)$ is finitely generated by
  `TauCeti.finite_singularHomology_of_finiteCWType` with
  `TauCeti.FiniteCWType.of_isManifold`.
- **Signs.** The flux is Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.Diffeotopy.flux`, with the
  page's sign. No Hamiltonian vector field or moment map of the page enters a
  statement. The sign of the loop transgression below is fixed by its
  definition through Tau Ceti's homotopy operator, and the flux formula
  states it once. Statement 4 asks only for membership in the group
  `Γ_σ`, so its sign does not matter there.

## Decisions

These settle the points that `DESIGN.md` leaves to this file.

1. **Trivializations over loops, not over the 1-skeleton.** The hypothesis
   of the milestone, that the restriction to every loop has a symplectic
   trivialization, is stated loop by loop. A symplectic trivialization
   along a smooth loop `γ` is a smooth `Φ : ℝ × F → M`, 1-periodic in time,
   over `γ`, whose slices read in any chart of `A` are in
   `diffeomorphStabilizer σ`. This is the pull-back of the fibration to the
   circle, trivialized. The page's statement 4 uses a trivialization over
   the 1-skeleton $B_1$ of a triangulation. Following the page's notes, it
   is read as a trivialization over some open set $U\supseteq B_1$. The
   design proves statement 4 in a form over loops. Then
   `OneSkeleton.lean` proves the page's form for a given smooth
   triangulation, with $\Phi$ defined on an open set containing $B_1$,
   and so the milestone in the page's form. Only that last file uses a
   triangulation.
2. **The homomorphism $f_\tau$ is read through holonomy in one chart.** Fix
   `b₀ : B` and the chart `e₀ := A.trivAt b₀`. For a symplectic connection
   with symplectic trivializations along every loop, the holonomy of a loop
   at `b₀`, read in `e₀`, lies in Tau Ceti's
   `TauCeti.SmoothForm.isotopyStabilizer σ`. Tau Ceti's
   `TauCeti.endpointFlux σ` sends it to $H^1(F;\mathbb R)/\Gamma_\sigma$.
   For a closed connection 2-form this is invariant under based homotopy,
   because then the holonomy around a contractible loop is Hamiltonian
   (*Coupling forms*). It is multiplicative under concatenation, so it is a
   homomorphism on `FundamentalGroup B b₀`, and `homologyLift` makes it
   $f_\tau$ on $H_1(B;\mathbb Z)$. Changing `b₀` or `e₀` composes $f_\tau$
   with an automorphism of $H^1(F)/\Gamma_\sigma$ induced by a
   symplectomorphism of `F` (Tau Ceti's `endpointFlux_mul_mul_inv` and
   `map_symm_mem_fluxGroup`). That changes neither statement 3 nor whether
   $f_\tau$ vanishes. The trivializations along loops enter only to show
   that the holonomy lies in `isotopyStabilizer` and to compute fluxes. The
   page's remark that changing the trivialization changes the flux by an
   element of $\Gamma_\sigma$ is the case of trivializations that agree
   with `e₀` at time 0. Every trivialization can be normalized so
   (`IsSymplecticTrivializationAlong.normalize`).
3. **Hamiltonian connections are characterized by their flux.** The kernel
   of `endpointFlux` is $\mathrm{Ham}$ (*Flux homomorphism*,
   `TauCeti.ker_endpointFlux`). So for a closed connection 2-form, $\Gamma_\tau$
   is Hamiltonian exactly when $f_\tau=0$. Loops at other base points are
   conjugate to loops at `b₀` by transport along a path, and
   `hamiltonianGroup` is normal among the symplectomorphisms. The criterion
   is `IsSymplectic.isHamiltonian_iff_holonomyFlux_eq_zero`.
4. **Route for 3 ⇒ 1: shift the connection by closed one-forms of the
   base.** The book changes $\tau$ by a closed form $\rho$, which it builds
   skeleton after skeleton of a triangulation of $B$. Tau Ceti has no
   theorem that a compact manifold has a smooth triangulation: it has
   `TauCeti.IsSmoothTriangulation` and steps towards Whitehead's theorem
   (`Geometry/Manifold/SmoothTriangulation/`), but no existence. So the
   design takes another route, which needs neither triangulations nor
   McDuff–Salamon's Proposition 10.2.19. Let $\widetilde f$ lift $f_\tau$.
   Take a basis $c_1,\dots,c_r$ of $H^1(F;\mathbb R)$, which is finite
   dimensional since `F` is compact. Write
   $\widetilde f=\sum_k\varphi_k\,c_k$ with homomorphisms
   $\varphi_k\colon H_1(B;\mathbb Z)\to\mathbb R$. By Tau Ceti's
   `TauCeti.deRhamCohomology.homologyPeriod_surjective`
   (`Geometry/Manifold/DeRham/PrescribedPeriods.lean`), each $\varphi_k$ is
   the period homomorphism of a closed one-form $b_k$ on $B$. Next choose
   one-forms $\alpha_k$ on $M$ whose restriction to each fibre is closed,
   in a class that is flat and equals $c_k$ at `b₀` (*Fibre classes*
   below). Then
   $$\tau'=\tau+\sum_k\pi^*b_k\wedge\alpha_k$$
   restricts to $\sigma$ on the fibres and is vertically closed: on two
   vertical vectors and any third one, $d\tau'$ is a sum of terms
   $b_k(v)\,d\alpha_k(\eta_1,\eta_2)$, and $d\alpha_k$ vanishes on the
   fibres. So $\Gamma_{\tau'}$ is symplectic, by the milestone of
   *Symplectic fibrations*. Its holonomy along a loop $\beta$ at `b₀`, read
   in a normalized trivialization, has the flux of that of $\Gamma_\tau$
   minus $\sum_k(\int_\beta b_k)\,c_k=\widetilde f([\beta])$ (the sign
   is that of `flux_holonomyDiffeotopy` below, where the flux is
   $-[\int_0^1\theta_t\,dt]$, and the added term adds to $\theta_t$, as
   in Decision 5). So its flux lies
   in $\Gamma_\sigma$, and $\Gamma_{\tau'}$ is Hamiltonian by Decision 3.
   $\tau'$ need not be closed. Statement 1 asks only for a Hamiltonian
   connection.
5. **Flux of holonomy through the homotopy operator.** For a vertically
   closed $\tau$ with symplectic $\Gamma_\tau$ and a symplectic
   trivialization `Φ` along a loop, write $\Phi^*\tau=\sigma+dt\wedge\theta_t$
   on $\mathbb R\times F$, with closed $\theta_t$. The holonomy read in `Φ`
   is the flow of the field $X_t$ with $\iota(X_t)\sigma=\pm\theta_t$, so by
   Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.Diffeotopy.flux_eq_mk_eulerianFluxForm` its flux is
   $\pm[\int_0^1\theta_t\,dt]$. This integral is Tau Ceti's
   `TauCeti.homotopyOperator` of the pull-back of $\tau$ to
   $F\times[0,1]$. The one formula gives two results. One is the shift of
   Decision 4: for $\tau+\sum_k\pi^*b_k\wedge\alpha_k$, $\theta_t$ gains
   $\sum_kb_k(\beta'(t))\,\Phi_t^*\alpha_k$, so the flux loses
   $\sum_k(\int_\beta b_k)\,c_k$.
   The other is the page's description of $\mu_{\Phi^*a}$ by integrals over
   tori, when $\tau$ is closed. For a closed $\tau$ the class depends only
   on $[\tau]$: by the homotopy formula
   `TauCeti.mextDeriv_homotopyOperator_add_homotopyOperator_mextDeriv` and
   periodicity, the operator sends exact forms to exact forms. So
   $\mu_{\Phi^*a}$ becomes a linear map on $H^2(M;\mathbb R)$,
   `loopTransgression`.
6. **Fibre classes.** Every holonomy is symplectically isotopic to the
   identity, so it acts trivially on $H^1(F;\mathbb R)$ (Tau Ceti's
   `TauCeti.deRhamCohomology.map_eq_of_homotopy`). Transport along paths
   from `b₀` therefore carries a class $c\in H^1(F)$ to a class in each
   fibre that does not depend on the path. Read in a chart, this class is
   locally constant. Glued with a partition of unity of $B$ subordinate to
   connected open subsets of the base sets, representatives give a one-form
   $\alpha$ on $M$ whose restriction to every fibre is closed and in that
   class. The transport used is that of $\Gamma_\tau$ itself, so no other
   connection is needed.
7. **1 ⇔ 2.** For 1 ⇒ 2, the radial trivializations of *Parallel transport*
   (`IsEhresmannConnection.exists_trivialization_parallelTransport`) for a
   Hamiltonian connection, centred at points `bᵢ`, are composed in the fibre
   with the transport along a fixed path from `b₀` to `bᵢ`, read in `e₀`.
   Their coordinate changes are holonomies around loops at `b₀`, so they
   lie in $\mathrm{Ham}$. For 2 ⇒ 1, take the connection 2-form of
   *Symplectic fibrations* built from a partition of unity and the charts
   of the reduced atlas. Read in one chart, it is a convex combination of
   the pull-backs of $\sigma$ along coordinate changes $g_i(b)$ that lie in
   $\mathrm{Ham}$ and are smooth in $b$. Along a curve $b(t)$ in that
   chart, $t\mapsto g_i(b(t))\,g_i(b(0))^{-1}$ is a smooth path in
   $\mathrm{Ham}$. So it is a Hamiltonian isotopy, by *Flux homomorphism*'s
   `isHamiltonian_of_forall_timeSlice_mem_hamiltonianGroup` (McDuff–Salamon,
   Proposition 10.2.12). The horizontal lift of the convex combination is
   the same convex combination of the velocity fields. Its contraction with
   $\sigma$ is exact at each time, so transport inside a chart is a
   Hamiltonian diffeotopy (the library's
   `isHamiltonian_iff_forall_interiorₗ_velocity_mem_exactForms`). Holonomy
   around any loop is a composite of such pieces and coordinate changes in
   $\mathrm{Ham}$. This is the route of the page's formalization note.
8. **Statement 4.** 3 ⇒ 4 takes $a=[\tau]$: for a loop whose class is
   torsion, $\widetilde f$ vanishes on it, so the holonomy flux, which is
   $\pm$ `loopTransgression Φ a`, lies in $\Gamma_\sigma$. For 4 ⇒ 3,
   Thurston's first step (*Compatible symplectic forms*,
   `ClosedExtension.lean`)
   gives a closed $\tau$ in the class $a$ that restricts to the fibre
   forms. The same formula shows that $f_\tau$ vanishes on torsion classes,
   and a homomorphism from a finitely generated abelian group to
   $H^1(F)/\Gamma_\sigma$ that vanishes on torsion lifts to $H^1(F)$
   (`AddMonoidHom.exists_lift_of_torsion_le_ker`).

## Files

### `Geometry/Symplectic/Fibration/LoopTrivialization.lean`

Symplectic trivializations along loops, and holonomy read in them. About
350 lines.

- `TauCeti.MapFiber.Atlas.IsSymplecticTrivializationAlong A σ γ Φ`, for
  `γ : ℝ → B` and `Φ : ℝ × F → M`: a structure with the fields
  `contMDiff` (`Φ` is smooth on `𝓘(ℝ).prod IF`), `periodic`
  (`Φ (t + 1, v) = Φ (t, v)`), `proj_apply` (`π (Φ (t, v)) = γ t`) and
  `mem_diffeomorphStabilizer`: for every `t` and `b` with `γ t` in the base
  set of `A.trivAt b`, some `φ ∈ diffeomorphStabilizer σ` has
  `((A.trivAt b) (Φ (t, v))).2 = φ v` for all `v`. With
  `IsSymplecticTrivializationAlong.diffeomorph t b`, that `φ`, chosen, and
  `coe_diffeomorph`.
- `TauCeti.MapFiber.Atlas.HasSymplecticTrivializationsAlongLoops A σ`: every
  smooth 1-periodic `γ` has a symplectic trivialization along it. This is
  the milestone's hypothesis. It is used by every result below.
- `IsSymplecticTrivializationAlong.normalize`: composing `Φ` in the fibre
  with the inverse of `hΦ.diffeomorph 0 b₀` gives a trivialization with
  `Φ (0, v) = e₀.symm (γ 0, v)`. Also `comp_add_const` (time shift) and
  `restrict`. If `γ` is constant near both ends of `[0, 1]`, a
  trivialization along the 1-periodic extension of `γ` on `[0, 1]` is one
  along any loop that agrees with it there.
- `IsSymplecticTrivializationAlong.holonomyDiffeotopy hΦ hD hsymp :
  TauCeti.Diffeotopy IF ∞ F`, for a symplectic connection `hD`
  (`IsEhresmannConnection.IsSymplectic`, *Symplectic fibrations*): at
  `(t, v)`, `Φ_t⁻¹ (hD.parallelTransport γ 0 t (Φ (0, v)))`. It is built
  with the library's `TauCeti.Diffeotopy.ofContMDiff`
  (`Diffeotopy/OfContMDiff.lean`). Smoothness comes from
  `parallelTransport_eq_timeDependentFlowOf` and Tau Ceti's smooth
  dependence of time-dependent flows. With `holonomyDiffeotopy_apply`,
  `timeSlice_holonomyDiffeotopy_mem_diffeomorphStabilizer`, and
  `final_holonomyDiffeotopy`: for a normalized `Φ`, the final map is
  `hD.parallelTransportInDiffeomorph e₀ e₀ γ 0 1`, the holonomy in `e₀`.
- `IsSymplectic.parallelTransportInDiffeomorph_mem_isotopyStabilizer`:
  under `HasSymplecticTrivializationsAlongLoops`, the holonomy of a loop at
  `b₀` read in `e₀` lies in `TauCeti.SmoothForm.isotopyStabilizer σ`.

### `Geometry/Symplectic/Fibration/LoopTransgression.lean`

The flux of a holonomy as a transgression. About 350 lines.

- `IsSymplecticTrivializationAlong.transgressionForm hΦ τ : SmoothForm IF F ℝ 1`:
  Tau Ceti's `TauCeti.homotopyOperator` of the rough pull-back of `τ.val`
  along `(v, t) ↦ Φ (t, v)` on `F × Icc 0 1`. With `transgressionForm_def`,
  and `transgressionForm_add` and `transgressionForm_smul` (it is linear in
  `τ`).
- `mextDeriv_transgressionForm`: closed when `τ` is closed, by the homotopy
  formula, since the two ends pull `τ` back to the same form by
  periodicity. `transgressionForm_mextDeriv_mem_exactForms`: the image of
  an exact form is exact.
- `IsSymplecticTrivializationAlong.loopTransgression hΦ :
  TauCeti.deRhamCohomology IM M 2 →ₗ[ℝ] TauCeti.deRhamCohomology IF F 1`,
  induced on classes, with `loopTransgression_mk`. Its period on a loop of
  `F` is the integral of the class over the torus
  $(t,s)\mapsto\Phi(t,\gamma(s))$, as on the page:
  `homologyPeriod_loopTransgression`, with Tau Ceti's `rectangleIntegral`
  (`Geometry/Manifold/DifferentialForm/Integration/Homotopy.lean`).
- `flux_holonomyDiffeotopy` (Decision 5): for `τ` that restricts to `σ`
  along `A` (`A.RestrictsTo σ τ`) and is vertically closed, and the
  connection `τ.horizontalDistribution π` of *Symplectic fibrations*, the
  flux of `hΦ.holonomyDiffeotopy` is
  `-TauCeti.deRhamCohomology.mk (hΦ.transgressionForm τ)`. That is the sign
  of the page's note, with the orientation of the torus as `rectangleIntegral` takes it.
  The proof finds the Eulerian field of the holonomy diffeotopy from the
  horizontal lift in `Φ` and applies `flux_eq_mk_eulerianFluxForm`.
- `flux_holonomyDiffeotopy_add_wedge` (the shift of Decision 4): for
  closed one-forms `b k` on `B` and one-forms `α k` on `M` whose pull-backs
  along every `Φ_t` are closed in a class `c k`, the holonomy flux of
  `τ + ∑ k, π^* (b k) ∧ α k` along `Φ` is that of `τ` minus
  `∑ k, (∫ b k along γ on [0, 1]) • c k`.

### `Geometry/Symplectic/Fibration/FluxHomomorphism.lean`

The homomorphism $f_\tau$ (Decision 2), and the Hamiltonian criterion. About
400 lines.

- `IsSymplectic.holonomyFlux hD e₀ γ :
  TauCeti.deRhamCohomology IF F 1 ⧸ fluxGroup σ`: the endpoint flux of the
  holonomy of `γ` read in `e₀`. It is defined by cases on membership in
  `isotopyStabilizer`, so that it needs no proof as an argument, with
  `holonomyFlux_eq` (the class of the flux of `holonomyDiffeotopy` for a
  normalized trivialization) and `holonomyFlux_eq_zero_iff` (the holonomy
  is in `hamiltonianGroup`, from `TauCeti.ker_endpointFlux`).
- `holonomyFlux_concat` and `holonomyFlux_reparam`: concatenation adds,
  and reparametrization does nothing (from `parallelTransport_comp` and
  `parallelTransport_parallelTransport`).
- `holonomyFlux_eq_of_homotopic`: for `τ` closed, homotopic loops at `b₀`
  have the same `holonomyFlux`. The holonomy around a contractible loop of a
  closed connection 2-form is Hamiltonian, by the theorem of *Coupling forms*
  (`Coupling.lean`) that a closed connection 2-form has Hamiltonian
  holonomy around contractible loops.
- `TauCeti.MapFiber.Atlas.fluxHomomorphism hA hS hL hτ hr b₀ :
  H₁(B; ℤ) →+ H¹(F) ⧸ fluxGroup σ`, for `τ` closed restricting to `σ`: the
  `homologyLift` of the homomorphism of `FundamentalGroup B b₀` that the
  previous items define. With `fluxHomomorphism_loopClass` (on the class of
  a smooth loop it is `holonomyFlux`). This is the page's $f_\tau$.
- `IsSymplectic.isHamiltonian_iff_holonomyFlux_eq_zero` (Decision 3), and
  for closed `τ`: `isHamiltonian_iff_fluxHomomorphism_eq_zero`.
- `AddMonoidHom.exists_lift_of_torsion_le_ker`: for a finitely generated
  abelian group `G`, an `ℝ`-vector space `V` and an additive subgroup `Λ`,
  a homomorphism `G →+ V ⧸ Λ` vanishing on `AddCommGroup.torsion G` lifts
  to `G →+ V`. The quotient of `G` by its torsion is free (Mathlib's
  `Module.free_of_finite_type_torsion_free'`), and a free group lifts
  (`Module.projective_lifting_property`). The converse is
  `AddMonoidHom.torsion_le_ker_of_lift`, since `V` has no torsion. Both
  are about Mathlib's types only, so they go in the namespace
  `AddMonoidHom` and in a file of their own,
  `GroupTheory/TorsionLift.lean`, in the same issue.

  Statement 3 does not depend on `b₀`, by the list itself, since 1 does not

  of [McDuffSalamon2017] adds that whether $f_\tau$ lifts is the same for
  every such `τ`. That is not part of the milestone, and the part does not
  prove it.

### `Geometry/Symplectic/Fibration/OneSkeleton.lean`

The page's form of statement 4. About 400 lines. Waits for the other files.

- A symplectic trivialization over an open set `U ⊆ B` is a trivialization
  `Φ : Bundle.Trivialization F π` with base set `U`, smooth with smooth
  inverse, whose coordinate changes with every chart of `A` lie in
  `diffeomorphStabilizer σ`. It is written out, with no definition:
  each coordinate change, at each point of `U` and of a base set of `A`,
  is equal to some element of that group.
  Each smooth loop in `U` inherits a symplectic trivialization along it
  (`isSymplecticTrivializationAlong_of_trivialization`).
- `Bundle.Trivialization.transgressionHom`: for `U` path connected, the
  homomorphism $\mu_{\Phi^*a}\colon H_1(U;\mathbb Z)\to H^1(F)$, the
  `homologyLift` of the loop transgressions. It is additive and invariant
  under homotopy of loops in `U`, since `Φ^*τ` is closed on `U × F`.
- `isHamiltonianFibration_iff_oneSkeleton`: for a smooth triangulation
  `f : Realization K ≃ₜ B` (`TauCeti.IsSmoothTriangulation`), with
  `B₁` the image of the 1-skeleton (Tau Ceti's
  `AlgebraicTopology/SimplicialComplex/Realization/Skeleton.lean`), an open
  `U ⊇ B₁` and a symplectic trivialization `Φ` over `U`, a class `a` with
  the fibre condition satisfies statement 4 exactly when
  $\mu_{\Phi^*a}$ sends every class of $H_1(B_1;\mathbb Z)$ whose image in
  $H_1(B;\mathbb Z)$ is torsion into $\Gamma_\sigma$. Every loop of `B` is
  homotopic to one in `B₁` by cellular approximation on the CW structure of
  the realization (`Realization/CWComplex/Basic.lean`, Tau Ceti's
  `exists_isCellular_homotopic`). Every class of $H_1(B_1)$ is a loop
  class, by the Hurewicz isomorphism `hurewiczHomologyAddEquiv`. With the
  list of `Characterization.lean`, this gives the milestone in the page's
  form, for some and for every such `Φ`:
  `isHamiltonianFibration_tfae_oneSkeleton`. Its docstring ends with
  "Milestone: *Characterization of Hamiltonian fibrations*."
